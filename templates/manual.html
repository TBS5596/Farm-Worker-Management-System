System Manual

Day-to-day operations: enrolling workers, verified clock-in, attendance, payroll, cameras and troubleshooting.

The one thing to understand first

A PIN on its own does not record attendance. When a worker clocks in, the camera compares the face in front of it with the face samples enrolled for that Worker ID. No match, no record. That is why enrolment is the setup step that matters most - a worker with no enrolled samples cannot clock in at all.

1) Setup Checklist, In Order
  1. Sign in as admin / admin and set a real password when asked.
  2. Settings - organisation name, farm coordinates, geofence radius, hourly rate, NAPSA and NHIMA rates, shift times, the pay cycle (weekly, fortnightly, twice a month or monthly), and Clock-in verification - what a worker must present to clock in (section 2).
  3. CCTV - add the clock-in camera, press Test, then the button to make it the attendance camera.
  4. Workers - add each worker with an hourly rate and a unique PIN.
  5. Biometric - enrol at least three face samples per worker.
  6. Have a worker clock in from the home page and check Attendance.
  7. Payroll - pick the cycle and a date in the period, and generate.
  8. Settings → Clock-in verification - choose what a worker must present to clock in (section 2). A fresh install starts at PIN + face.
  9. Optional: Settings → Worker cards for what the barcode carries, then Workers → Issue cards and print the sheet (sections 5 and 6).
  10. Tell workers they can check their own hours and payslips at /me on their phones (section 11).
2) Clock-in Verification: What The Terminal Checks

Settings → Clock-in verification. One setting decides what a worker must present at the terminal, and therefore what your attendance record is worth. Three checks are available.

CheckWhat it isWhy it counts
CardSomething the worker has Says who is being claimed, before anything is typed
PINSomething the worker knows Four digits, typed in front of a queue
FaceSomething the worker is The only one that cannot be handed to a friend

Pick the combination from the list on the settings page. Each option shows which of the three it uses and what it gives up.

SettingUse it when
Card, PIN and face The strongest. Wherever attendance feeds payroll without a supervisor standing at the terminal
PIN and face Before cards have been printed. A shared PIN still records nothing, because the face must match
Card and face A busy gate. Faster, and still safe — the card says who is claimed, the face decides whether to believe it
Card and PIN, no camera Weak Both can be handed to a friend
PIN only, no camera Weak A demonstration setting
Card only Weak Whoever holds the card is recorded as present

About the three weak settings

They exist because a demonstration on a laptop with no working camera needs one. They are marked Weak and say plainly what they give up, because a farm that quietly stays in one of them has an attendance record that no longer means what everybody assumes it means. If you switch to one for a demonstration, switch it back afterwards. Two things make that hard to forget: the Dashboard shows what is being checked in its System Readiness list, in amber if a check is off, linking straight back to the setting; and every change is written to the Audit Log by name.

A card method needs cards. Choosing a card option does not issue anybody one — do that on the Workers page (section 5). A worker not yet issued a card can still clock in with their worker number, and the audit log records that the card was not used.

3) Worker Onboarding

Example: add a new worker named "Moses Banda".

  1. Go to Workers and click Add Worker.
  2. Enter name, phone, NRC, department and hourly rate; set status to active.
  3. Leave Pay Cycle on Farm default unless this worker is paid on a different cycle from the rest of the farm.
  4. Create a unique PIN (at least 4 digits).
  5. Click Save Worker. A 4-digit Worker ID is generated automatically.
  6. Go to Biometric and enrol his face before his first shift.

4) Face Enrolment

On the Biometric page each worker has a row showing how many samples are stored. Ask the worker to stand square to the camera in even light, with no hat or sunglasses, then press the green camera button on their row. Repeat three to five times with small changes of angle and expression.

  • 3 / 8 green - enrolled and reliable.
  • 1 / 8 amber - enrolled but too few samples; matching will be unreliable.
  • Not enrolled - cannot clock in.

No camera to hand? Use the button to enrol from clear front-facing photographs instead. The button deletes all samples for that worker so you can start again.

5) Worker Cards

Optional. Scanning a card says who is standing here before any PIN is typed, so a clock-in can check all three things at once: the card the worker has, the PIN they know, and the face they are. Which of the three actually run is one setting - Clock-in verification, section 2 - rather than three separate switches.

SideWhat is on it
Front The worker's photograph, name, Worker ID and department, so a supervisor can hold the card up and see whether it belongs to the person in front of them. The photograph is not a separate upload — it is one of the face samples enrolled on the Biometric page, the very image the camera checks against at clock-in. A worker who is not enrolled gets a grey outline instead, and the cure is enrolment.
Back The barcode, the code in readable type, and — deliberately — the worker's name and ID again in small print. Section 6 of this page explains why that small print matters more than it looks.

The one decision worth thinking about

A card gets dropped, lent and left in pockets, so what is printed on it matters. Settings offers three choices, and the default is the first only because it is what farms usually ask for.

OptionWhat is printedWhat it costs
The NRC itself (default) The worker's National Registration Number Anyone who finds the card and scans it with a free phone app reads a national ID number
A one-way scramble of the NRC A code such as FMS-4A9C21-B0E7F3 The NRC cannot be read back out of it, but the code is fixed by the NRC, so a lost card cannot be given a genuinely new number
A generated card number A code such as FMS-K7P2-M4XQ Means nothing outside this system. The only option where a lost card can be voided and replaced cleanly

Issuing. On the Workers page, Issue card on one row, or Issue cards to everyone for every active worker who does not already have one - it never overwrites a card already in somebody's pocket. Void retires a lost card without touching the attendance recorded against it. A worker with no NRC on record cannot be issued a card under either NRC option; the system says so by name.

Printing. Workers → Print cards, then read the next section before you press Print.

Scanning. A USB scanner needs no driver: it behaves as a keyboard, typing the code and pressing Enter. The computer's own camera can also be used with Scan with camera on the clock-in screen, which needs Chrome or Edge on a desktop - on browsers that cannot do it, the button does not appear at all. The code can always be typed by hand, which is what you do when a scanner fails at six in the morning.

6) Printing Cards Without Mixing Up The Barcodes

What can go wrong, and why it is expensive

A card has a photograph on one side and a barcode on the other. If those two are ever paired wrongly — Musonda's face on the front, Mwiza's barcode on the back — then every time Musonda clocks in, the hours are recorded against Mwiza. Nobody notices at the terminal, because the scan works perfectly. It surfaces weeks later as a payroll dispute, by which point the attendance record is wrong and contains nothing to say why.

What the system does about it. Every card back is printed with the worker's ID and name on it in small type. A back that names its owner cannot be quietly attached to the wrong front — you can see the mismatch. That small print is the whole safeguard.

Method 1: Fold — the safe one

Press Fold at the top of the print page. Each worker's two sides print next to each other on one piece of paper.

  1. Load card stock or thick paper.
  2. Set Scale to 100%. Not "Fit to page", not "Shrink to fit" — a resized barcode will not scan.
  3. Set Two-sided / Duplex to off. Both sides are already on one face of the paper.
  4. Print one page first and look at it before printing the rest.
  5. Cut around the solid outer line. Each card comes out as one wide piece.
  6. Fold along the dashed centre line, so the photograph and the barcode end up back to back. Glue or laminate.

Why this is the safe method: both sides of a card are printed next to each other and cut out together, so a barcode physically cannot end up on somebody else's card. If you are printing for the whole farm at once, use this.

Method 2: Double-sided — neater, if your printer is reliable

Press Double-sided. Photographs print on one page, barcodes on the next, and you get a single-thickness card rather than a folded one.

  1. Load card stock or thick paper.
  2. Set Scale to 100%, as above.
  3. Set Two-sided to Flip on long edge (some printers call this "Long-edge binding" or "Book"). Not short edge — that turns the backs upside down.
  4. Print one sheet only first. Set the page range to 1-2.
  5. Hold that sheet up to a window or a lamp. On every card, the small name and ID on the back must be the same person as the photograph on the front. If any card disagrees, stop — your printer is flipping on the wrong edge or reversing the page order. Switch to Fold, which cannot go wrong that way.
  6. Only once a test sheet checks out, print the rest.
  7. Cut around the solid outline of each card.

Do not skip step 5. It takes ten seconds, and it is the only thing standing between a mis-feeding printer and a payroll dispute.

Before you hand them out. Fan through the finished cards and check a few at random: the face on the front and the name on the back should agree. Thirty seconds on a batch, and much cheaper than finding out from a payslip.

8) Attendance Records
  • Identity - a green badge with the match score means the face matched the enrolled template.
  • Location - "On site", or the distance if outside the radius.
  • Clip - opens the short video recorded at that punch.
  • Rebuild Daily Summaries - recalculates hours, overtime and lateness for the last 30 days.
  • Export CSV - the whole table for a spreadsheet or report.
9) Analytics

Dashboard → Analytics. The Attendance page says what happened; this page says what it means. Every figure is computed from the attendance and payroll records - nothing is estimated. If a number here disagrees with the attendance register, the register is right and that is a fault worth reporting. Choose the period at the top right: 14, 28, 56 or 90 days.

The questionWhat it showsWhat to do with it
Is attendance still being verified? Sessions confirmed by the camera, entered by hand, and refused - with the reasons ranked A falling verified share usually means a camera is struggling, not that workers are cheating. Look here first when other numbers go odd
Who is not coming to work? Workers ranked urgent, look or watch, each with what was observed and a suggested action A name on the register with no attendance behind it is how a ghost worker starts. Ask about a pattern; ignore a bad week
Where is the wage bill going? Total cost for the period, split by department and by worker, with each worker's share Watch for pending payroll rows - those figures can still change
When is the work happening? Hours by day of the week split into ordinary and overtime, plus average day length per department A worker whose day length sits well outside the rest usually means short shifts or unclosed sessions, not anything deliberate

When the fault is the setting, not the worker

If most of the workforce is flagged late by a similar small margin, the page says so plainly and points at the shift start time in Settings rather than at the workers. Correct the setting and the flags clear. The worker's own page carries the same note, so nobody is accused of lateness that a setting invented.

Keeping it current. The selector at the top of the page can reload it every 30 seconds, minute, 5 minutes or 15 minutes - useful on an office screen left open all day. The choice is remembered for that browser only, so one screen can refresh every minute while a manager's laptop does not refresh at all. The farm-wide starting value is set in Settings, and the page does not reload while nobody is looking at the tab.

10) Payroll

Example: pay the week ending Sunday 23 August.

  1. Go to Payroll. Leave Pay cycle on the farm default unless you are paying a different cycle.
  2. Set Date in the period to that Sunday.
  3. Click Generate Payroll.
  4. Check the rows, then export the CSV as the payment list.
  5. After paying, edit each row and set status to paid with the payment date. Marking a period paid is also what releases the payslip to the worker's phone.
Choosing the period

The farm can be paid weekly, fortnightly, twice a month or monthly, set on the Settings page. An individual worker can be put on a different cycle from their record on the Workers page - casual labour weekly and permanent staff monthly is a common arrangement.

CycleWhat the date you choose means
WeeklyThe last day of the week you are paying
FortnightlyThe last day of the fortnight
Twice a monthAny day in the half-month. It fills in 1-15, or 16 to month end
MonthlyAny day in the month. It fills in the whole month

Only workers on the cycle you chose are paid. Running the monthly period does not touch the weekly casuals, and the message afterwards says how many were left alone for that reason.

Hours come from the recorded sessions; gross pay, NAPSA, NHIMA and net pay are calculated from the worker's rate. Periods already marked paid are never overwritten, so re-running is safe. A hand-entered correction still has its pay calculated - you enter hours and a rate, never an amount.

If you see a red "already paid" message

A worker is skipped when a different paid period already covers some of these days. That happens when somebody moves from weekly to monthly: their first four weeks are already settled. Nothing went wrong - the system stopped a double payment. Either mark the old period unpaid, or generate from the date after it ends.

11) For Workers: Your Own Hours and Payslips

Workers can look up their own records without asking anyone. This is separate from clocking in, and it gives no access to the office pages.

Example: Musonda checks last month's payslip on his phone.

  1. On the farm WiFi, open the clock-in screen and tap My hours & payslips at the bottom (or go to /me).
  2. Type Worker ID 0001 and his PIN.
  3. Tap Take a photo and take a photo of his face.
  4. Tap Sign in.

Signing in takes three things: the Worker ID, the PIN, and a photo taken there and then.

Why the photo

Anyone who watched a worker type their PIN at the terminal still cannot open their payslips, because the system checks the face against the one enrolled for that Worker ID. A PIN was only ever meant to guard a clock-in; it is four digits, and it gets typed in front of a queue. A worker whose face is not enrolled cannot sign in here at all - enrol them on the Biometric page first.

TabWhat it shows
Home Whether they are clocked in right now, hours and days this week and this month, what they have earned so far this pay period and what that is on course to become, their hours over the last four weeks against the four before, their usual longest day, their own details, their pay cycle, and their last payslip
Attendance Every shift recorded for them, newest first, with the photo taken at clock-in
Payslips Each paid period, with hours, overtime, rate, gross pay, NAPSA, NHIMA and net pay
My record The last eight weeks as four figures - hours, days, punctuality, earnings - a chart of hours per week, and what they were paid in each closed period
My card Their own identity card on screen with a Print my card button, so somebody who has lost theirs can print a replacement without waiting for the office. Shown only when cards are switched on

Everything here is read-only. A worker cannot see any other worker, cannot reach any office page, and cannot change a single figure. Corrections are made by a supervisor on the dashboard.

About the earnings figure on Home

It is worked out from recorded hours at the worker's rate, before deductions, and the page labels it an estimate - the payslip is the figure that counts. It is shown anyway because "how much have I earned this week" is the question workers actually ask, and an honest estimate answers it better than silence does. Where the farm's shift start time is set earlier than work really begins, the punctuality note on this page says so rather than presenting the lateness as the worker's fault.

Why the current period is missing. A period appears only once it has been marked paid. Until then the figure can still change when payroll is regenerated, and showing a number that later moves causes more disputes than it settles.

If somebody is locked out. Five wrong attempts in fifteen minutes locks that Worker ID for fifteen minutes. Waiting clears it, and so does a supervisor resetting the PIN on the Workers page.

16) Routine
  • As it starts: read the two lines it prints. They name the records file it opened and how many workers and attendance records are in it. If it ever says NEW AND EMPTY when you expected yesterday's work, stop and read those lines rather than re-entering anything — the records are almost certainly in the other file, and the message says where.
  • Weekly, and before anything risky: copy the records file (fms.db). On a farm host it is one power cut away from being the only copy.
  • Each morning: CCTV → Health Check; confirm the attendance camera is online. While you are on the Dashboard, glance at Clock-in checks in System Readiness — it should be green. Amber means a check has been switched off.
  • During the day: watch Attendance; refused attempts and their reasons appear on the Biometric page.
  • End of each pay period - Friday on a weekly cycle, month end on a monthly one: Payroll → choose the cycle and a date → generate → export → mark paid. Marking paid also releases the payslip to the worker's phone. If the farm runs two cycles, run each one separately.
  • Once a week: open Analytics and read the four sections. Two minutes, and it is where a ghost worker, a failing camera or a wrong shift-start setting shows up before anyone complains.
  • New worker: add on Workers, then enrol at least three samples, and issue a card if the farm uses them. Without enrolment they can neither clock in nor use the worker portal.
  • Worker leaves: deactivate on Workers and void their card. Records stay for the audit trail; they can no longer clock in.
  • Card lost: void it on the Workers page first, then issue and print a replacement. Voiding is what stops the old card working; printing a new one on its own does not.
  • After printing a batch of cards: fan through them and check a few at random that the face on the front and the name on the back agree (section 6).
