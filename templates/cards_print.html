{# A printable sheet of two-sided worker identity cards. Deliberately standalone rather than extending the admin layout: this page exists to come out of a printer, so it carries no sidebar, no navigation and no colour that would drain a farm office's ink. The on-screen toolbar and the instructions are hidden by the print stylesheet. Cards are laid to 85.6 x 54 mm, the same footprint as a bank card, so a farm can print onto card stock and cut, or print on paper and laminate into a standard badge holder. FRONT the worker's photograph, name, worker ID and department BACK the barcode, the code in readable type, and - deliberately - the worker's ID and name again in small print That last detail is the safeguard against the one mistake that matters here. A sheet of anonymous barcodes can be cut or collated wrongly and nobody notices until a worker is refused at the gate, or worse, until one worker's attendance is recorded against another's name. A back that says whose back it is cannot be silently attached to the wrong front. Two layouts, because the two ways of printing a two-sided card fail differently: fold front and back side by side. Cut the pair out as one piece, fold down the middle. Any printer, no alignment to get wrong. duplex fronts on one page, backs on the next, with the backs mirrored so they land behind the right fronts when the paper is flipped on its long edge. A proper single-thickness card, but it depends on the printer feeding straight. #} Worker cards - {{ org_name }}

How to print these — fold method

  1. Load card stock or thick paper.
  2. In the print dialogue set Scale to 100%. Not "Fit to page", not "Shrink to fit" — a resized barcode will not scan.
  3. Set Two-sided / Duplex to off. These cards carry both sides on one face of the paper.
  4. Print one page first and check it before printing the rest.
  5. Cut around the solid outer line of each card. Each card comes out as one wide piece.
  6. Fold along the dashed centre line so the photograph and the barcode end up back to back, then glue or laminate.
Why this method is the safe one. Both faces of a card are printed next to each other and cut out together, so a barcode physically cannot end up on somebody else's card. If you are printing for the whole farm at once, this is the method to use.
{% else %}
Read this before printing double-sided. This layout puts the photographs on one page and the barcodes on the next. If your printer pulls the paper through even slightly out of order, a worker's card ends up carrying somebody else's barcode — and their clock-ins would then be recorded against the wrong person. Every back is printed with the worker's ID and name on it precisely so you can catch that, but you have to look. If you are not sure about your printer, use Fold instead.

How to print these — double-sided method

  1. Load card stock or thick paper.
  2. In the print dialogue set Scale to 100%. Not "Fit to page" — a resized barcode will not scan.
  3. Set Two-sided to Flip on long edge (some printers call this "Long-edge binding" or "Book"). Not short edge, which turns the backs upside down.
  4. Print one sheet only first. Set the page range to 1-2 and print that before anything else.
  5. Hold that sheet up to a window or a lamp. On every card the small name and ID printed on the back must be the same person as the photograph on the front. If any card disagrees, stop: your printer is flipping on the wrong edge, or reversing the page order. Switch to Fold, which cannot go wrong that way.
  6. Only once a test sheet checks out, print the rest.
  7. Cut around the solid outline of each card.
Every back carries the worker's ID and name in small print at the top. It is there for step 5 — a back that names its owner cannot be quietly attached to the wrong front.
